01

Breakeven decay: how fast the profit goes to zero

The question the review asked for directly. Sort every delivery day from best to worst, then delete the best day, the two best, the three best… and watch what is left of the P&L.

Breakeven days
How many of the best days you must delete before the entire P&L is gone. The line's crossing of zero. Two days means two lucky sessions are the strategy; 184 days means the earnings are spread across most of the sample.
Why % of active days
A strategy that only trades 79 days cannot be compared with one that trades 250 on raw day counts. Switch the x-axis to normalise by each strategy's own active days.
Why not Sharpe
Sharpe is mean ÷ SD. Removing a big positive day cuts the mean once and the SD twice, so on right-skewed P&L Sharpe rises as you truncate. The Sharpe view below shows that perverse behaviour on purpose — P&L retained is the honest measure.

02

The coin-flip trader

Ten thousand traders who take the same clips on the same products at the same timestamps and flip a coin for the direction. If the strategy cannot separate itself from that crowd, its returns are the timestamps, not the signal.

Null A
Same trades, same products, same clock, side randomised. 10,000 runs. This is the pure direction test.
Read at mid
The coin flip is scored at mid, where friction is off both sides so the null sits on zero. Score it after costs and the null goes deeply negative — beating it then only proves you churned less than a random trader, which is not skill.
z-distance
(observed Sharpe − null mean) ÷ null SD. The seed-stable statistic. The best of 10,000 figure moves by up to 1.5 between random seeds, so it is context, never the verdict.
Thresholds
Keep z ≥ 4 and the best-of-10,000 verdict holds across five seeds · Suspect z 2.5–4, or that verdict flips with the seed · Fail z < 2.5 or p > 0.01.

03

Bootstrapped Sharpe and Sortino

A single Sharpe number is one draw. Resample the daily P&L 10,000 times and the interval tells you what the sample can actually support.

Bootstrap
Draw 546 days at random with replacement from the strategy's own days, 10,000 times, and recompute the statistic. The 2.5th–97.5th percentile band is the 95% interval.
Block bootstrap
Same, but resampling contiguous 8-day blocks so day-to-day autocorrelation survives the resample. Where the two intervals disagree, the wider one is quoted.
t-statistic
Point estimate ÷ bootstrap standard error. The Newey–West t on mean daily P&L is shown alongside in the table — it corrects for serial correlation instead of resampling.
Thresholds
Keep lower bound > 1.0 · Suspect lower bound 0 to 1.0 · Fail the interval contains zero.

04

Deflated Sharpe and its breakeven trial count

The one number in the pack that prices the search itself.

What deflated Sharpe actually is

If you try 100 strategies on the same 18 months and report the best one, its Sharpe is high partly because it won a lottery. Deflated Sharpe (Bailey & López de Prado) asks the honest question: given that N attempts were made, what is the probability this strategy's true Sharpe is above zero? It docks the raw number for three things at once — how many attempts were made, how short the sample is, and how skewed and fat-tailed the daily P&L is (a few enormous days flatter Sharpe far more than they should). Read it as a probability: above 0.95 the edge survives the search; below 0.95 the headline Sharpe is inside what searching alone would have produced.

Breakeven trials
The number of attempts at which the curve falls through 0.95. Compare it with the count the submitter declared. If breakeven is below declared, the strategy is dead on the submitter's own admission — no further argument needed.
Two variance assumptions
The maths needs the variance of Sharpe across the trials that were run, and the records do not contain it. Analytic assumes the trials differ only by sampling noise — generous. Cross-sectional uses the observed spread of daily Sharpe across the seven submissions (v = ) — harsh. Both are plotted; the verdict uses the generous one.
Thresholds
Keep breakeven ≥ 10× declared (or never breaks) · Suspect 1× to 10× declared · Fail breakeven below declared.

05

Baseline comparison

Three benchmarks that need no signal at all. If a strategy cannot beat them, whatever it found was in the market, not in the model.

Always long / short
The same trades, same products, same timestamps, same clips — direction forced one way. Isolates the direction call from the selection and timing. For a strategy that is 100% long anyway, always-long is the strategy — that is information, not a bug.
Matched passive
10 MW long on every product in the window, entered at the strategy's own median entry snapshot and held for its median holding period. The fair clock-matched comparison, because the book drifts up per 15-minute step and a longer hold collects more of it for free.
Full-window buy-and-hold
10 MW long on every product from the first book row to the last: snapshot 0 (delivery start − 10h30m) to snapshot 39 (delivery start − 45m). Snapshot 39 is the last tradeable row, fifteen minutes before gate closure at start − 30m — nothing in the pack reaches gate. It lifts the ask on entry and hits the bid on exit, so it pays the same round trip everyone else does; it just pays it once over 9h45.
Breadth-neutral edge ratio
Edge per MWh ÷ what holding the whole market over the same clock window earned per MWh, at mid. Sharpe rewards the passive benchmark for holding 24 products a day when a selective strategy holds one; this ratio removes that advantage. Keep ≥ 3× · Suspect 1–3× · Fail below 1×, or always-long beats the strategy.

How to read this and what it does not do

Round trips are recovered from the blotters by FIFO matching per delivery product and repriced against the order book; nothing here uses strategy source code. Every claimed P&L, Sharpe, Sortino, drawdown and hit rate reproduces exactly at the mid basis, which is what makes the fee and spread deltas trustworthy. Sortino follows the submitters' own definition — sample SD of negative days only — because that is what reproduces their claimed figures.

  • Execution bases. mid = (bid+ask)/2 both legs, no fee — a price nobody quoted. passive_in_fee = entry rests at mid, exit crosses, fee both sides. cross_fee = buy the ask, sell the bid, fee both sides — the honest rung.
  • Slippage is modelled as spread crossing, not as market impact. Book depth is a uniform 10 MW at each of three levels and every clip is 10 MW (20 MW for PINGPONG), so walking the book differs from crossing only for PINGPONG.
  • Not modelled: fill probability, adverse selection, market impact, and imbalance settlement — there is no imbalance price in the data.
  • One correction to the earlier audit. Its baseline table priced the strategy leg at mid while pricing always-long, always-short and buy-and-hold at the selected rung. Every comparison on this page prices both sides at the same rung.
