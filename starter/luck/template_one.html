Breakeven decay: how fast the profit goes to zero

Sort every delivery day best to worst, then delete the best day, the two best, the three best… and watch what is left. One line per execution basis, so you can see friction pull the breakeven in.

Breakeven days
How many of the best days must be deleted before the entire P&L is gone — where the line crosses zero. Two days means two lucky sessions are the strategy.
Why % of active days
The count on its own means little without knowing how many days were traded at all. The second x-axis normalises by this strategy's own active days.
Why not Sharpe
Sharpe is mean ÷ SD. Deleting a big positive day cuts the mean once and the SD twice, so on right-skewed P&L truncation raises Sharpe while the money disappears. The Sharpe view shows that on purpose — P&L retained is the honest measure.

02

The coin-flip trader

Ten thousand traders who take exactly these clips on these products at these timestamps and flip a coin for the direction. If the strategy cannot separate itself from that crowd, the returns are the timestamps, not the signal.

Null A
Same trades, same products, same clock, side randomised. 10,000 runs.
Null B
Same delivery days, same trip count per day, same holding periods — but product, entry snapshot and side all drawn at random from that day. A harder null: it removes the hour selection too.
Read at mid
The verdict is scored at mid, where friction is off both sides and the null sits on zero, so the distance is pure directional skill. On a rung that charges friction the null goes deeply negative and beating it only proves the strategy churned less than a random trader.
z-distance
(observed Sharpe − null mean) ÷ null SD — the seed-stable statistic. The best of 10,000 figure moves between random seeds, so it is context, never the verdict.

Bootstrapped Sharpe and Sortino

A single Sharpe is one draw. Resample this strategy's own daily P&L 10,000 times and the interval shows what the sample can actually support — on each basis.

Bootstrap
Draw its days at random with replacement, 10,000 times, and recompute. The 2.5th–97.5th percentile band is the 95% interval.
Block bootstrap
The same, resampling contiguous 8-day blocks so day-to-day autocorrelation survives. Where the two disagree, the wider interval is quoted.
t-statistics
Point estimate ÷ bootstrap standard error, with the Newey–West t on mean daily P&L alongside — that one corrects for serial correlation instead of resampling.

04

Deflated Sharpe and its breakeven trial count

The one number that prices the search itself.

What deflated Sharpe actually is

If you try a hundred strategies on the same eighteen months and report the best one, its Sharpe is high partly because it won a lottery. Deflated Sharpe (Bailey & López de Prado) asks the honest question: given that N attempts were made, what is the probability this strategy's true Sharpe is above zero? It docks the raw number for three things at once — how many attempts were made, how short the sample is, and how skewed and fat-tailed the daily P&L is (a few enormous days flatter Sharpe far more than they should). Read it as a probability: above 0.95 the edge survives the search; below 0.95 the headline Sharpe is inside what searching alone would have produced.

Breakeven trials
The number of attempts at which the curve falls through 0.95. Compare it with the count this submission declared — . If breakeven is below declared, the strategy is dead on the submitter's own admission.
Two variance assumptions
The maths needs the variance of Sharpe across the trials that were run, and the records do not contain it. Analytic assumes the trials differ only by sampling noise — generous. Cross-sectional uses the observed spread of daily Sharpe across the seven submissions (v = ) — harsh. Both curves are drawn; the verdict uses the generous one.

05

Baseline comparison

Benchmarks that need no signal at all. If the strategy cannot beat them, whatever it found was in the market, not in the model.

Always long / short
Its own trades, products, timestamps and clips with the direction forced one way. The gap between the strategy and always-long is the entire value of the direction call.
Matched passive
10 MW long on every product in the window, entered at this strategy's own median entry snapshot () and held for its median holding period. The clock-matched comparison, because the book drifts up per 15-minute step and a longer hold collects more of it for free.
Full-window buy-and-hold
10 MW long on every product from the first book row to the last: snapshot 0 (delivery start − 10h30m) to snapshot 39 (start − 45m). Snapshot 39 is the last tradeable row, fifteen minutes before gate closure at start − 30m — nothing in the pack reaches gate. It lifts the ask on entry and hits the bid on exit, so it pays the same round trip everyone else does; it just pays it once over 9h45.
Breadth-neutral edge ratio
Edge per MWh ÷ what holding the whole market over the same clock window earned per MWh, at mid. Sharpe rewards a passive benchmark for holding 24 products a day when a selective strategy holds one; this ratio removes that advantage.

How to read this and what it does not do

Round trips are recovered from this strategy's blotter by FIFO matching per delivery product and repriced against the order book; nothing here uses strategy source code. Every claimed P&L, Sharpe, Sortino, drawdown and hit rate reproduces exactly at the mid basis, which is what makes the fee and spread deltas trustworthy. Sortino follows the submitters' own definition — sample SD of negative days only — because that is what reproduces their claimed figures.

  • Execution bases. mid = (bid+ask)/2 both legs, no fee — a price nobody quoted. passive_in_fee = entry rests at mid, exit crosses, fee both sides. cross_fee = buy the ask, sell the bid, fee both sides — the honest rung.
  • Slippage is modelled as spread crossing, not market impact. Book depth is a uniform 10 MW at each of three levels.
  • Not modelled: fill probability, adverse selection, market impact, and imbalance settlement — there is no imbalance price in the data.
  • Baselines are priced on both legs at the same basis as the strategy they are compared with.
  • Two figures come from the whole cohort by construction and cannot be computed from one submission: the cross-sectional trial variance behind the harsh deflated Sharpe, and the breadth-neutral edge ratio's market denominator. Both are noted where they appear. Everything else on this page is this strategy alone.
